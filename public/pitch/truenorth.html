Section one

What we found.

First, what this is not. It is not a critique of your ice cream or your shops. You make everything in-house, you grew from a home kitchen into two shops, and when Marshall opened, the wait for a scoop stretched past an hour. The product is not the problem. The problem is that the website undersells it, and the case it makes daily behind your counter never reaches anyone online.

Also worth saying: some things your site gets right. The phone numbers are tap-to-call, the flavor lists carry allergen marks, and the menu publishes real prices. Plenty of local sites do none of that. What follows is what is costing you customers, verified on August 17, 2026, with a link on every claim so you can check it yourself.

Search results show hours that are wrong

Your homepage tells search engines: "Made daily in our shop in Marshall, MI. Open 2-9 pm every day." Your own pages say noon to 9. That grey line under your name in Google is the first thing a customer reads, and at 1pm on a Saturday it tells them you are closed.

Shared links show no photo

Your site has no share image configured. Paste truenorthicecream.com into a Facebook post or a text message and the preview comes up with no picture. Every recommendation a happy customer makes online arrives looking like a fax.

Try it: paste your web address into Messenger and watch the preview

The flavors page does not know what is in the case

Rotating flavors are your whole story, about 30 in the case at a time out of roughly 45 a month. The flavors page is a text-only list, and your site's own sitemap records it last modified February 1, 2025. The page even says "stop by often or ask about our latest offerings." The website is where people ask.

Catering has no way to book

Your highest-value line of business, weddings and corporate events of three hundred guests and more, offers a phone number and truenorthicecream@gmail.com. No form, no way to capture an inquiry at 11pm when someone is planning their wedding on the couch.

Battle Creek barely exists online

Your second shop gets a phone number and an address line. The contact page maps only Marshall, and your site title and description mention only Marshall, so nothing on the site presents Battle Creek as a real, findable location with its own map and hours.

Small things that read as neglect

Your sitemap records the About page last modified October 2024 and Contact November 2024. And the flavors page spells it "Strawberry Lemondade." None of this is fatal. All of it says nobody is minding the store online, which is the opposite of how you actually run your shops.

Section two

What we built.

We did not bring sketches. The rebuild is deployed and you can open it on your phone right now. Your logo, your photos, your menu, your prices. Three things worth trying:

The flavor board

"In the Case Today" lists exactly what is scooping, in your four categories, with allergen badges and a visible last-updated date. Updating it is simple: text us the new lineup and it is live the same day, covered by your monthly. Or do it yourselves, and we will show you how: the board is one plain list you can edit from a phone, and the site rebuilds itself about a minute after you save. Either way the answer to "what do you have today" is finally on the website.

A catering funnel that never closes

Event type, date, guest count, straight to your inbox with the customer's email a reply away, at 2pm on a Tuesday or midnight on a Saturday. The per-person price table is right next to it, so inquiries arrive pre-sold. There is also a proper contact form for everything else.

Two shops, told properly

Both locations with maps, tap-to-call numbers, live open-right-now status, and the structured data that tells Google there are two True North shops with the correct noon-to-9 hours. Share the site anywhere and a real photo shows up. And watch the compass in the logo while you scroll.

Worth a conversation: online ordering

Your cakes and pies are made to order and your pints leave the shop by the armful, and right now none of that can be ordered ahead. If you want it, we build pre-orders for cakes, pies, and pints straight into the site with Stripe or Square checkout. No ordering platform, no monthly platform bill, no percentage skimmed beyond the card fee. That is Custom Order territory and we would scope it together, but say the word and it is on the table.
